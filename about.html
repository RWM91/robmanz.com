About

The line isn't straight

My resume has a gap in it, and I've stopped trying to hide it.

I started at the University of Cincinnati, not a school venture firms recruit from. So I made my own way in. I founded Bearcat Ventures, a student fund that raised $1.2M to back UC alumni startups. I also led UC's first team to a global VC competition. That led to an internship at Refinery Ventures, then a role as an investment analyst at R1 Capital. There I wrote memos that won more than $2M in funding and worked alongside a founder we'd bet on, helping them find product-market fit and figure out who their ideal customer really was.

Then, in 2024, I stepped away for personal reasons. For nine months the career plan came second.

When I came back, I didn't wait for the perfect role. I joined Wholesale Auto Gear and did whatever the business needed. I used AI tools to cut month-end close time in half, and I launched a content strategy that brought in 75% of inbound leads. I also finished Kellogg's product management certificate.

When my own job search got messy, I treated it like a product problem. I started with a Notion workspace. When I outgrew it, I built Ladder — a career search-focused SaaS CRM using Claude Code. It's deployed and hosted, and connected to Gmail, Google Calendar, Zoom, Granola and WisprSync, plus an open-source AI companion interface. I built it for myself, but other people in the same spot wanted it too. For the first time I wasn't judging whether a product deserved to exist. Real users were showing me that it did.

That's the trajectory I'd ask you to judge me on. I spent years deciding which bets deserved funding. Now I want to build them.
